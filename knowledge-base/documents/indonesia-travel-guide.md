# Indonesia Travel Guide

## Overview
Indonesia is the world's largest archipelago nation with 17,000+ islands, 270 million people, and extraordinary biodiversity. It spans Southeast Asia and Oceania, straddling the equator.

## Best Time to Visit
- **Dry Season (April–October)**: Best for most of Indonesia. Clear skies, ideal for beaches, hiking, and diving.
- **Wet Season (November–March)**: Heavy rainfall, especially December–February. Some areas flood. Fewer tourists, lower prices.
- **Bali**: Year-round destination. Dry May–September is peak. Ubud is pleasant year-round.
- **Komodo**: Best April–August for diving; avoid December–February (rough seas).

## Top Destinations

### Bali
- Ubud — arts, culture, rice terraces, and monkey forests
- Seminyak & Kuta — beach clubs, surfing, nightlife
- Uluwatu Temple — clifftop temple with Kecak fire dance at sunset
- Tegalalang Rice Terrace — UNESCO-listed stepped rice paddies
- Mount Batur — popular sunrise trek (1,717m), 2–3 hour hike

### Jakarta
- National Monument (Monas) — 132m tall, panoramic city views
- Old Batavia (Kota Tua) — Dutch colonial architecture
- Thousand Islands (Kepulauan Seribu) — day-trip snorkeling and beaches
- Grand Indonesia & Plaza Indonesia — luxury shopping malls

### Yogyakarta
- Borobudur Temple — world's largest Buddhist temple (UNESCO World Heritage)
- Prambanan Temple — 9th-century Hindu temple complex (UNESCO)
- Kraton (Sultan's Palace) — seat of Yogyakarta Sultanate
- Mount Merapi — active volcano, trekking and jeep tours

### Komodo Island
- Komodo National Park — home to Komodo dragons (UNESCO World Heritage)
- Pink Beach (Pantai Merah) — one of only 7 pink sand beaches in the world
- Manta Point — world-class manta ray diving/snorkeling
- Best visited May–August

### Raja Ampat (West Papua)
- Globally recognized as the most biodiverse marine area on Earth
- 600+ coral species, 1,700+ fish species
- Remote and pristine — requires internal flight to Sorong then boat
- Best for diving and snorkeling (October–April)

## Getting Around
- **Domestic Flights**: Lion Air, Citilink, Garuda Indonesia. Jakarta–Bali ~$30–80.
- **Trans-Jakarta Bus**: City bus network, flat fare ~$0.30.
- **Gojek & Grab**: Ride-hailing apps, cheapest way to get around cities.
- **Ojek**: Motorcycle taxi, negotiable price.
- **Ferries**: Inter-island travel, various operators.
- **Renting Scooter in Bali**: ~$5–8/day, international license recommended.

## Local Cuisine
- **Nasi Goreng**: Fried rice, Indonesia's national dish
- **Rendang**: Slow-cooked beef in coconut and spices (West Sumatra)
- **Gado-gado**: Peanut sauce salad with tofu and vegetables
- **Sate Ayam**: Chicken satay with peanut sauce
- **Bakso**: Meatball soup, popular street food
- **Gudeg**: Young jackfruit curry, Yogyakarta specialty
- **Mie Ayam**: Chicken noodle soup
- **Babi Guling**: Balinese suckling pig (non-halal)
- **Warung**: Local warungs (small restaurants) serve meals for $1–3

## Practical Information
- **Currency**: Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). 1 USD ≈ 16,000 IDR.
- **Language**: Bahasa Indonesia (official). English widely spoken in tourist areas.
- **Religion**: Predominantly Muslim (87%), Hindu majority in Bali.
- **Dress code**: Cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples. Sarongs often provided.
- **Bargaining**: Expected at markets, not in malls or restaurants.
- **Electricity**: 220V, Type C/F plugs.
- **Safety**: Generally safe for tourists. Watch for bag snatching in cities.

## Visa Information
- **Visa on Arrival**: Available for 96 countries at major airports. $35 USD for 30 days, extendable once.
- **Visa Free**: Citizens of 9 ASEAN countries including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand.
- **Indonesian citizens** do not need visa within ASEAN.
